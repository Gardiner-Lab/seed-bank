What it holds

Lines GL-L-0001

A genetic identity: a wild type, a mutant, a transgenic event, a cross. With its parents, its background and the plasmids it carries. A line does not change from one generation to the next.

Seed lots GL-S-0001

One bag of seeds: one line, one generation, one source. Where it is kept, how much is left, its germination and zygosity, and segregation tests on selection plates.

Plants GL-P-0001

One plant sown from a lot and numbered, where and under what treatment it grew, and what became of it: harvested, crossed, or the founder of a new line.

Phenotypes

What you saw, scored against named traits so it can be compared between plants, lines and treatments, with photos, 3D scans and files.

Family trees

Any record's whole descent, drawn across the suite: from the plasmid and its glycerol stocks, through every generation, to the plants growing now.

Labels

Bag and pot labels with QR codes: a phone camera on one opens the record, which is how a pot in a greenhouse says what it is.

The cycle

The three records connect in a cycle, and that cycle is the lineage:

seed lot— sow → plants— harvest one → next generation— sow → …

Because every lot remembers the plant it came off, and every plant the lot it was sown from, nothing has to be reconstructed later from memory. A cross joins two plants; founding a line starts a new identity from one plant.

Codes

Every record has a code to write on a bag or a pot: the lab's own code, a letter for what it is, then its number.

GLthe lab SL line · S seed lot · P plant 0042its number in that lab

The lab's lead chooses the lab code before the lab's first record; after that it is fixed, no two labs share one, and a code is never reused. Lab Stocks uses the same lab code, so GL-S-0001 (a seed lot) and GL-G-0001 (a glycerol stock) belong to the same lab.

With the other tools

  • Plasmid Editor — the plasmids a line carries, with their maps and sequencing reads.
  • Lab Stocks — the glycerol stocks of those plasmids, and plant material taken from a plant here.
  • Lab Notebook — type @ to link a line, lot or plant. The record then lists the pages that mention it.
  • MoClo Library Tool — where the plasmids were assembled.

The family tree reaches across all of them, which is what makes "where did this line come from?" a question with an answer.

Who sees what

  • Your lab sees and changes its own records.
  • The other labs of your chair group see them, but cannot change them — which is how a cross with a neighbouring lab's mutant is possible without handing over your records. Chair groups are set by an administrator on the dashboard's Labs tab.
  • Other labs see nothing of yours.

Records that others descend from cannot be deleted, so a lineage never loses its middle.

Where the data is kept

On the lab's own server, in one database with the photos and 3D scans inside it, backed up each night with the rest of the suite.
